FINITE COLLECTION COMPARISON: MATHEMATICAL SPECIFICATION

This is an independent reduced-model comparison. It does not reproduce
the finite-wire boundary-element code of code/finite_tile/ or assume its pickup-spacing law.
The main note explains the physical conclusion. This file specifies the
new port operator and the approximations used to translate its result.

1. Geometry, polarization and normalization

The plasma occupies 0<x<L, 0<y<W and a wire length H=0.10 m in z.
The static field and wires point along z. We keep the z-independent E_z
sector with PEC walls in x and y. The end-wall condition is compatible with
this sector. A physical probe localized along z would need a further 3D
calculation; here each pickup is an optimistic extended column along z.

The orthonormal transverse basis is
  phi_mn(x,y) = (2/sqrt(L W)) sin(m pi x/L) sin(n pi y/W).
The dimensionless uniform-source coefficients are f_mn=8/(pi^2 m n) for
odd m,n and zero otherwise. Symmetric, equal-phase pickup arrays only couple
the odd-odd sector. The finite basis captures the relevant near-resonance
field; omitted high-order modes have small response, as checked by refinement.

Define omega0=2 pi nu0, k0=omega0/c, z=omega/omega0, p=omega_p/omega0,
and gamma(z)=Gamma_p(z)/omega0. The copper damping obeys
  Gamma_p(z) = R_s(z nu0)/(mu0 r ell),
  ell = ln[a/(2 pi r)] + F_square, F_square=0.52734... .
The starting square-lattice pitch is selected with r/a=0.03 and
  omega_p^2 = 2 pi c^2/(a^2 ell).
The starting plasma frequency compensates the PEC fundamental cutoff:
  p_base^2 = 1 - [(pi/L)^2+(pi/W)^2]/k0^2.
We subsequently retune p at fixed wire damping so the loaded resonance
peaks at omega0. This is effective-medium tuning, not an independently
calibrated formula for the required microscopic wire displacements.

Q_* = omega0/[Gamma_p(1) p_base^2] is a fixed normalization reference.
The actual unloaded material Q after retuning is
  Q_u = omega0/[Gamma_p(1) p^2].
The response quality factor is a different quantity: peak frequency/FWHM.
The code records Q_reference, Q_unloaded, Q_response and the plasma retuning.

2. Maxwell operator and a passive coherent receiver

With E/E0 = Q_* sum_j e_j phi_j sqrt(LW), the dimensionless modal operator is
  D_j(z) = Q_* [q_j^2/k0^2 - z^2 + p^2 z/(z+i gamma(z))],
  q_mn^2=(m pi/L)^2+(n pi/W)^2.
The source is z^2 f. A single receiving mode with unit-normalized spatial
profile u has a separable load
  [D + z (alpha-i zeta) u u^dagger] e = z^2 f.
Here alpha is a real reactive load and zeta>0 is dissipative extraction into
the receiver. These are dimensionless network parameters. In a multimode
device zeta is not in general the external/internal decay ratio of one mode.
The anti-Hermitian part of the added operator is negative semidefinite:
the load is passive under the exp(-i omega t) convention.

The profile is the normalized sum of K equal-phase Gaussian columns,
  u(x,y) proportional to sum_j exp[-((x-x_j)^2+(y-y_j)^2)/w^2].
The columns are placed at regular cell centers. All allowed tested grid
shapes are listed in each result. The nominal width is w=2a, and w=a,4a
are checked at 0.5,1,2 meV. A normalized continuous profile u=f/|f| is a
separate optimistic distributed receiver. None of these is claimed to be
the best possible probe shape or an actual combiner implementation.

There is one output bath even for K>1. A lossless coherent network therefore
gets the benefit of nonlocal collection without an invented K-fold detector
noise penalty. Its routing and fabrication cost are not estimated.

The rank-one inverse is evaluated exactly. Let
  G=u^dagger D^-1 u, H=z^2 u^dagger D^-1 f,
  t=H/[1+z(alpha-i zeta)G].
Then u^dagger e=t and
  e=D^-1[z^2 f-z(alpha-i zeta)u t].
The output power boost relative to a bare mirror of area W H is
  b_port = k0 L Q_* zeta |t|^2.
Alpha and zeta are numerically searched, and the plasma is then retuned at
fixed chosen load to center the loaded resonance. All reported peaks are
at the nominal comparison frequency to the frequency-grid precision.
This is an optimization within the stated family, not a proof of a global
optimum over antenna designs, geometries and all passive matching networks.

The source-work identity is
  Im[(z^2 f)^dagger e] = sum_j[-Im D_j]|e_j|^2 + z zeta |t|^2.
Both terms on the right are nonnegative. The code evaluates this identity
at each optimized peak. An independently assembled sparse finite-difference
Helmholtz operator provides a second field-solution check.

3. Radiation and statistical comparison

The radiative model uses vector mode matching for the discrete impedance
sheets and a free-space aperture radiation admittance. Propagation between
sheets is explicit. The radiative admittance and Gaussian port are held at
the nominal frequency across each spectrum; the existing model's prior
frequency and modal checks justify that narrow-resonance approximation.
The present calculation independently refines modes and quadrature at 1 meV.
At 4 meV, a paraxial-admittance high-mode check raises the central integral
by about 2% between 60 and 240 modes; the 120-to-240 change is about 0.41%.
At 60 modes the paraxial and full-admittance peak powers differ by 0.0034%.
The highest-mass numbers should therefore be read at few-percent numerical
precision, separately from their larger hardware-model uncertainties.
The aperture model is not wire-surface-resolved and uses an infinite flange.

The common metric is I=integral b(z)^2 d ln z over 0.85<z<1.15. Treating
local tuning spectra as translated profiles gives the scan-rate ratio
  R = (I_rad/I_port) (eta_rad/eta_port)^2 (S_port/S_rad)^2.
S denotes output noise spectral density. At fixed observing time the
coupling ratio is R^(-1/4). Numerical g, B, density, area and total live time
cancel in this relative comparison. They are not varied to produce a win.

For a background-dominated, single-output counting comparison with equal
dark backgrounds, the integral between the half-maximum crossings is also
recorded. That is an optimally centered one-FWHM rectangular signal filter;
for an asymmetric spectrum its center need not equal the peak. The adopted
hardware settings are those searched with the full-band metric, so the
counting comparison is not independently optimized. No new absolute
photon-counting reach is claimed. The earlier QCD-target forecast is unchanged.

4. Halo correction

For each receiver, average the squared reciprocal overlap with
exp(i k_a . x), k_a=(omega0/c^2) v, over Gaussian velocities. The transverse
y wind is 232 km/s, with dispersion 220/sqrt(2) km/s along all axes.
For the port, the reciprocal modal field is proportional to D^-1 u and the
spatial transform factorizes. The uniform z profile contributes sinc(k_z H/2).
For the radiative model, the depth-integrated reciprocal E_z coefficients
are obtained from the actual passive Gaussian illumination. Phase across
its thin depth is neglected. Gauss-Hermite velocity integration gives the
mean received power. The peak power factor chi multiplies each narrow-band
integral as chi^2. Its variation across the port FWHM is below 0.5% in the
checked cases. A full directional, time-dependent halo scan is beyond this
comparison, as is a joint exact spatial and frequency line-shape treatment.

5. Downstream transport and available volume

The optional guide loss is a matched attenuator AFTER an ideal combiner.
It is not modeled as distributed loss inside the coherent pickup network.
This distinction preserves the assumed loading/backaction. Actual lossy
combiner branches could change both extraction and noise and require a new
multiport model. The guide uses TE10, a_w/b_w=2 and nu/nu_cutoff=1.7.
Its surface resistance is initially the same assumed copper law as the
converter; half that resistance doubles the parity length. The optical
power transmission 0.8 is an explicit benchmark, not a measurement.

At equal receiver noise the parity power loss is
  Loss_port_dB = -10 log10[eta_rad sqrt(I_rad/I_port)].
All paths must be cold and filtered consistently with the detector noise
budget. Alternative guides, free-space output and different receiver
placement can avoid the illustrative loss. No actual ALPHA chain is assigned
these losses. The available-depth calculation allows the port to occupy
30 mm while retaining one radiating face for the grid design. It is not a
global optimization of a solenoid, folded optics or multiple radiating faces.

6. Appropriate use

This calculation supplies a falsifiable engineering target for a prototype:
measure the delivered overlap, response width, collection and transmission
at equal usable magnetic volume and receiver resources. Its strongest
conclusion is conditional advantage in specified sparse-collection or
transport-loss regimes. It does not prove novelty over all literature,
universal superiority over ALPHA, or demonstrated QCD-axion sensitivity.
