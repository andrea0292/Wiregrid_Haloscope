**ReuterSondheimer:1948** — NOT ACCESSIBLE

- Claim: 'In cold, pure copper the electron mean free path (micrometers) far exceeds the classical skin depth (tens of nanometers at THz frequencies), so we use the extreme anomalous skin-effect limit, in which the surface resistance is independent of the dc resistivity and grows as ν^{2/3}'.
- Where: RS 1948 full text not accessible. royalsocietypublishing.org returned a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403) even after the domain was allowlisted; no OA copy found. Only the Crossref abstract was seen, so no equation number in RS can be given. Secondary source used for the content: Ratzinger & Wang, arXiv:2211.00135v3, p. 10, Eqs. (42a)-(42d) and (44) (R∞ ∝ f^(2/3); RS coefficients); p. 2, Eq. (4) and Table I (mean free path).
- Quote: RS abstract: "relaxation effects are negligible in the microwave region but become important in the infra-red and eventually restore the validity of the classical theory"
- Note: What the RS abstract establishes: an exact solution valid at all frequencies and temperatures; at large mean free path the field propagates as a 'surface wave' that differs from the classical exponential; Pippard's simplified theory is qualitatively correct. The abstract does NOT give the ν^(2/3) law or the statement that R_s does not depend on ρ. Secondary support (arXiv:2211.00135v3, p. 10): Chambers' interpolation for copper is R = 1.123e-9 f^(2/3) [1 + F_R α^(-G)] (Eq. 42a), with RS-fitted coefficients F_R = 1.004, G = 0.333 (Eq. 42d) and α = 1.5 h0^(-2) linked to RS Eq. (19). As α → ∞ (extreme anomalous limit), R → 1.123e-9 f^(2/3), independent of σ. Physically the result depends only on ρl = m v_F/(n e^2), a material constant: ρl ≈ 6.6e-16 Ω m^2 (my calculation from the Table I parameters of 2211.00135). Mean free path: the same paper gives l(4.2 K) = 11.7 µm for RRR = 400 copper (p. 2, Eq. 4), so 'micrometers' is supported. Skin depth: with σ(4.2 K) = 1.77e10 S/m from that paper, my calculation of the classical skin depth gives 15 nm at 60 GHz, 7.6 nm at 250 GHz, 3.8 nm at 1 THz and 2.7 nm at 2 THz (zero field). 'Tens of nanometers at THz' therefore overstates it; magnetoresistance at 10 T raises this somewhat. Caveat (my estimate; flagged by the RS abstract): at these frequencies ωτ ≈ 2-60 (τ = 4.9 ps), so relaxation is not negligible. The extreme-anomalous ν^(2/3) law still applies while v_F/ω (≈4 µm at 60 GHz, 0.13 µm at 2 THz) is much larger than the field penetration depth, which holds across the bands considered. The RS citation should not be read as covering this regime without comment. Bib entry matches Crossref (Proc. R. Soc. A 195(1042), 336-364, 1948).

Optionally cite Ratzinger & Wang, arXiv:2211.00135, for the 4.2 K copper parameters.
- Source read: Crossref metadata and abstract for RS 1948. Full text: arXiv:2211.00135v3 (secondary). Crossref verification of Chambers 1952.
